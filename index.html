201) Existential risk demonstrator (website)

See link for more info: https://villeseppala.wordpress.com/2025/07/07/website-concept-for-visualizing-existential-risk-looking-for-feedback-funding/

In short, the site allows the users to input values for existential risk probability for various time periods (or survival probability at given points). It then calculates the expected longevity of humankind, measured in expected years the humankind will live or in expected human lives left. The graphs show the results in both the condensed and linear time scales. The site demonstrates how very small improvements in existential risk in our lifetime will pay off immensely in the long run.

202) How should we evaluate the threat levels of specific existential risks? (Text)

(medium size text, possibly an article)

Related to project 201.

Most studies that concern individual existential risks ask the respondents to estimate the probability of them causing humankind’s extinction or a civilisational collapse. There are major issues with this approach, sometimes noted in the literature. Risks are intertwined and it is also difficult to determine what is the threshold of contribution from a specific threat for it to be considered as a cause for an extinction/catastrophic event. To illustrate: If an extinction event is caused by a misaligned AI using nuclear weapons, is it an AI or nuclear extinction? Most of the time in the literature it is allowed to be both. But if AI is used only very partially, in a very mundane capacity, does it still count? Climate change poses even bigger complexity. It is very unlikely that climate change in itself would cause extinction, but it may increase the probability of major conflicts and therefore, it could be a major factor behind extinction even if it wasn’t the final mechanism for it. But often in the literature it seems that this is not taken into account in the threat estimates from climate change.

Toby Ord explores these issues to some extent in Precipice (2020) and talks about risk factors that influence the risks themselves. I would like to explore this further and consider if it would be wise to evaluate the threat from specific risks by asking how much the removal of a threat (risk factor) altogether would reduce the overall existential/catastrophic risk probability. The classification of these . This estimate would indicate the overall impact of a threat (regardless of how deeply entrenched the threat is), while taking into account the impact from other threats (avoid double counting). Focus on risk factors and their reduction would also be a more policy relevant approach.

I think that there are two ways to estimate the risk reduction probability. Firstly, in percentage points, as absolute reduction. In this case, if a threat was totally uncorrelated to other threats (such as m maybe asteroid impacts), the reduction estimate would also correspond to the standard risk probability estimate from the risk.

Second way is to estimate the reduction relative to the overall risk probability, a percentage reduction than a percentage point reduction. This could be preferable especially if the overall risk is hard to estimate but the relative magnitude of the specific risk relative to others, and the risk interaction to relative to them, is clearer. Questionnaires could be built to accomodate both ways.

203) Overlapping tariff unions as a global political tool (text)

(medium size text, possibly article)

I feel that when punitive tariffs are used, they are usually based on general disagreements between countries. They might lead to a chain of retaliatory tariffs and worsen relations between the countries even more.

Encouraging countries to set tariffs based on specific issues instead of antagonism could be more productive. The tariff setter would have to specify a measurable topic the tariff is based on and could not hide behind ambiguity. This would also let the world to better evaluate how fair the tariffs are. The tariff receiver would know what to change in order to get the tariffs taken off or reduced. This would make the tariffs to be guiding. An example of this kind of tariff is the EU’s carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM) starting in 2026.

Encouraging this kind of tariffs could help to move the world from blocs and into overlapping tariff unions on various topics. Setting tariffs would become less dramatic, more business as usual. This could relieve retaliatory pressure. This would lower the threshold to set tariffs and bring the tariff topics more into international and national political discussion.

Not sure yet if it would be optimal to let countries set within unions to set tariff rates separately, or would it be better if the rates were equal.

207) Incentivizing built of new electricity interconnectors across Europe (text)

(Shorter text, a blog)

Problem and motivation

Sweden and Norway (hereafter, the North) have shown resistance to building new power interconnectors to Central Europe. (links here). They would increase the average electricity prices in these countries, since there is more demand for electricity in the Central Europe. However, they would benefit Europe as a whole. The population weighted decrease in electricity prices in Central Europe would be greater than the increase in the North.

Decreasing electricity prices across Europe is important for Europe’s industrial competetiveness.

Could EU or the European countries within the same power market create a system to encourage building new interconnectors?

My suggested solution (in progress)

My idea for this: Countries who import electricity at any moment would pay to the countries that export it to them? The size of payment would be determined by the contrafactual electricity prices for the case that there was no interconnector between the countries. This indicates the benefit of the interconnector to the importer and the harm to the exporter. The payment would not be 100% of the difference, since the exporting country benefits by having a greater power generation industry to tax. And because their own prices increase less than is the decrease in importing country.

This soluction would also incentivize countries to remove bureaucratical barriers from building new electricity generation if it is cheaper than payments to importers. Would decrease average electricity prices.

This would incentivize building flexible power generation wherever possible, since it would fetch a better price than currently. Which would decrease average prices.

The momentary wind energy production varies a lot more across Europe than across a single country. Stronger power connections between countries would help to balance this variation. This would make wind energy capture rates be higher. It is important that we can take the max. benefit from wind energy, since it is so cheap to produce.

They would incentivize countries to build steady production electricity, since that would decrease their dependence from exporting countries when electricity is expensive.
